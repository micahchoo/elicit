Lesson 2 · How Elicit Works

How a Question Gets Made

One win today: trace, from memory, the order Elicit tries things in to find your next question. Six research passes went into reading the actual control flow rather than trusting doc prose — and the headline is that the docs' tidy list of "question sources" is not the order the code runs. This lesson walks the real order.

Eight words first

These eight carry the whole lesson. Skim them now; they're one click away in the plain-English glossary whenever a later lesson uses them cold.

Snippet a saved passage of your own words — exact, dated, never edited again. The atom.
Sitting one sit-down with the app ("session" in code).
Queue the durable to-ask list that survives between sittings.
Bank a hand-curated file of opener questions — the fallback of last resort.
Red Light something in your last answer that invites a follow-up: an odd word, a person named but unexplained, an abstraction with no memory under it.
Resonance the every-turn search of your archive for past snippets that echo or clash with what you just said.
Sounding a consent-gated chain of questions, each built from your previous answer — drilling down, 8–12 steps.
Shadow mode a mechanism that runs and logs what it would do, without doing it — this project's rollout discipline for anything that acts on its own judgment.

The real order, one live turn

Every turn after the first, userTurn (src/elicitor/elicitor.ts:324-513) tries the following in order and stops at the first thing that produces a question:

  1. Almost out of time? (:375) — within two questions of the sitting's budget, everything below is skipped and the closing questions begin.
  2. Already mid-descent? (:382) — if a Sounding (or its cousin the DRM, below) is active, it owns the turn. Nothing else gets considered until the descent ends or you park it.
  3. Was your last answer a shrug? (:401) — a flat, closed answer ("not sure") is detected by a simple heuristic and treated as "change tack, don't push": the app skips composing anything and jumps straight to drawing a waiting question (step 7's fallback).
  4. Does the archive have something to say? (:409) — Resonance search runs every turn. A hit becomes a juxtaposition: your words now and your words then, quoted side by side, dated — "same thing?" First juxtaposition to survive the guard wins the turn.
  5. Any Red Lights? (:443) — only if no juxtaposition survived: the app scans your answer for a Red Light and composes a follow-up around your exact phrase.
  6. The ordinary probe (:462) — no signal anywhere? Compose a generic next question for whatever question-style is active, with one retry if the guard rejects it.
  7. The floor (:494, :507) — if even the retried probe fails, draw from the Queue, then the Bank, and if both are empty, a fixed hardcoded question. The turn never comes up empty-handed.
Opening a sitting uses a different order (startSession, elicitor.ts:126-137): your requested shuffle first, then the Queue, then the Bank. Don't conflate starting a conversation with continuing one — they're separate code paths.

Nothing composed reaches you unguarded

Steps 4–6 all share one shape, and it's the single most reusable idea in this codebase: the model composes freely, then a mechanical guard — plain string checks, no model — decides if the result is allowed out. The guard can only reject, never fix. For a Red Light follow-up (src/clerk/composed.ts, src/elicitor/guards.ts), the checks are:

  • the quoted phrase is an exact substring of what you typed — a model may have spotted the Red Light, but if the phrase it flagged isn't literally in your answer, it's dropped, no exceptions
  • the question adds something — it can't just parrot your fragment back
  • your words appear in quotes or on their own line, visibly separate from agent prose
  • it's actually a question
  • no "I think" / "I feel" from the agent outside the quoted span

Fail twice and the turn falls to the next step in the order — a worse question that's certainly safe beats a better one that might be putting words in your mouth. You'll see this compose-freely/guard-mechanically shape again wherever the model generates anything.

Where the waiting questions come from

Step 7's Queue isn't a dumb list — background work keeps it stocked. Three stockers worth knowing, each explained in plain terms:

Gap-fill: asking about recorded holes

When a fragment of yours fails the save-worthiness tests — say it can't be understood without its conversation — it's kept anyway as a Bud, with the failure recorded. Each recorded failure is a ready-made question ("who is 'she' here?"). A background sweep (src/clerk/gap-fill.ts) walks the Buds and mints those questions into the Queue. The same sweep also catches half-Constructs: if you said you value being "disciplined" but never named its opposite pole, the app asks "what's the opposite of this, for you?" — because in the psychology this borrows from, a personal dimension isn't defined until both poles are.

Grep for "gap-fill" and you'll actually hit four systems. Besides the one above: empty slots in an essay's arrangement (handled in clerk/arrangements.ts, minted only when you pick that draft), and two "what haven't we talked about" checklists — territory skeletons and atlases (src/ktg/) — which track only asked-or-not per topic and are barred from ever computing a score or telling you "you've never mentioned X." Same name, four different holes.

Still-true revisits: aging statements get re-asked

A background job rotates through Snippets older than 90 days and mints a fresh question quoting one — worded differently than the original ask, so you're answering the question again rather than recognizing your old answer. One flipped answer is treated as noise, not a contradiction.

The Randomizer: shuffle, never invent

To keep the model of you from overfitting to well-trodden territory, a shuffle mechanism resurfaces forgotten Snippets — picking a time-band uniformly first (so a quiet five-year-old memory competes evenly with a loud recent one), then a sitting, then a snippet — or draws from decks you curated. It contains no model call at all (a test literally greps the file for model-calling words), and when you trigger it, the agent cannot veto the draw.

Drawing from the Queue: never the single best

When the Queue is consulted, draw() (src/queue/queue.ts) filters what's askable right now — does it fit your declared time and energy, is it not parked, is its moment right — sorts survivors with your own declared questions first, then takes the top three and picks one at random (pickTopK, queue.ts:65). Never just the top one. That's a deliberate, tested rule (the register calls it "never argmax"): a deterministic picker would develop favorites, and favorites shape the archive.

The two instruments

Step 2 mentioned descents. There are two, and the contrast teaches the shape of both:

SoundingDRM
What it isa chain — each answer becomes the seed of the next questiona fixed routine — list yesterday's episodes, then probe each one the same four ways (where, what, who with, how it felt)
Directiondrills down into one belieftiles across one day
Lengthunknown in advance; 8–12 steps, ends at a cap or when your answers start circling backbounded by how many episodes you listed
Ends becausea structural check — never because a model judged you "done"the list runs out

Both use the same three-way consent gate — continue / park it / another day — and parking keeps all depth: the descent waits in the Queue until you explicitly resume it. Two details in the DRM are locked design decisions, not accidents: the feelings question is asked open ("How did that time feel?") with guidance offered only if your answer comes back flat, and episode listing takes start times only — asking people to estimate durations is rounding theater.

Built but not yet live

Several mechanisms above exist in full, tested code and are still in shadow mode — evaluating and logging every turn, acting on none of it, until the log earns them a graduation:

MechanismStatus
Question-variety balancing in the Queue draw (by Facet — the kind of self-knowledge each question targets)Shadow, behind an env flag — held back from the 2026-08-03 graduation; its own record shows it never changes a draw while pool entries carry no facet stamp
Pattern-derived questions (composed via registered content-free moves like "suppose…", "what would X look like in ten years?")Gate live (graduated 2026-08-03) — inert until the docket wiring lands (111 remainder)
Atlas-driven "we haven't talked about…" questionsLive (graduated 2026-08-03) — mints capped openers, one per region ever
System-triggered (unrequested) Randomizer drawsLive (graduated 2026-08-03); your own shuffle button was always live
Red Lights, juxtapositions, still-true revisits, Bud gap-fillLive

The shadow-vs-live dividing line is itself a design rule worth knowing (register entries Q-35 and Q-62): anything that would act on its own judgment shadows first; anything that only offers — where ignoring it means nothing happens — ships live immediately.
