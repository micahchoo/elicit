## 1. Hypothesis

From the plan, Task 16: *a local model can produce a valid op list often enough
that the wiki accretes claims worth reading, and the mechanical layers hold
when it cannot.*

The eval's findings #1, #3 and #6 predicted the second half would carry the
weight — that the interesting measurement would be the failure machinery
catching a model that could not hold the contract.

**The result inverts that prediction, and the inversion is the finding.** The
model never once failed the contract: 144 op proposals, 144 accepted, zero
rejected, zero unparseable, zero empty, zero oversized. The failure machinery
was never invoked because there was no failure to invoke it. What did fail was
something no one had instrumented — a call that never returned at all (§13),
against which "reject and back off" has nothing to say, because backing off
requires the call to end.

And the second half of the hypothesis — *claims worth reading* — is where the
honest answer is no, for a reason that has nothing to do with the model's
compliance. See §7 and §15.

## 4. Headline — op rejection rate by reason

**Zero. 0 of 144.**

SOURCE: `jq -r .op vault/wiki/sweep-log.jsonl | sort | uniq -c` → `144 MINT`,
and every one of the eleven `wiki-run` lines reads `rejected=0`. There is not
one `claim-op-rejected` line in the log.

The rejection rate was chosen as the headline because it answers *can this
model hold the six-op write contract*. The measured answer needs a
qualification the number alone hides:

**The model held a one-op contract 144 times. It never exercised the other
five.** All 144 ops are MINT. No UPDATE, no MERGE, no SUPERSEDE, no ARCHIVE,
no KEEP was proposed once. A perfect score on a contract where only one branch
was ever taken tells us the schema is easy to satisfy; it does not tell us the
model can choose among six. That measurement is still absent, and it is absent
for a structural reason rather than a modelling one — see §7.

The plan's rules 4 and 5 (KEEP is the safe default; rejected ops are logged and
skipped) were therefore never tested against real model output. They remain
tested only by fakes.

### The stall

**The 20:00:19Z run wedged on one mint call for 76 minutes.**

SOURCE: the log's last line before the gap is `threshold-clipped ... clipped=72`

**Nothing on our side ever gave up.** The embeddings path had a 120s

## 15. The read — essay or pile?

**A pile, and the corpus can prove why rather than merely assert it.**

Read from the store, not from the rendered route: no server was running when
this document was written. To read what the route serves I reproduced its
ordering from `src/server.ts:1241-1280` — group by facet in `FACETS` order, sort
within a facet by `coreness` descending, ties broken by id.

That reproduction turned out to be exact, for a reason that is itself the
finding. `coreness` (`src/wiki/status.ts:379`) measures a claim's 2-hop
neighbourhood through *shared cited snippets*. No two claims in this corpus
share a snippet (§7). Every claim's neighbourhood is therefore itself, alone;
every score is identical; the tie-break carries the whole ordering.
**The reading order is id order, which is mint order, which is the order the
readings happened to sit in the sweep queue.**

The wiki is 144 disconnected nodes sorted into eight labelled buckets. There is
no ranking because there is nothing to rank on, and no argument because no two
sentences touch the same evidence.

Individually the sentences are good. Here is the whole `value` facet, in the
order a reader would meet it:

> The user enjoys color bending, considering it both a form of visual protest
> and a stimming activity. *[in their personal practice]*
>
> **Prioritizes listening to others' existing desires over imposing their own
> voice.** *[when collaborating with others]*
>
> The user considers modifying accessible hardware to be inherently
> educational. *[when working with cheap, hackable devices]*
>
> The user considers "Readings" to be their primary area of interest.
> *[generally]*
>
> The user holds that reducing friction is a worthwhile goal but cannot by
> itself provide adequate orientation for users. *[in platform design
> philosophy]*
>
> The user prioritizes archivable and annotable interfaces to create spaces for
> future reflection. *[in interface design]*
>
> The person acknowledges that alternative operational approaches can hold
> merit. *[when assessing operational alternatives]*
>
> The user considers co-designing and co-writing metaphors an essential part of
> their work. *[at work]*

**The passage that decides it is the second one**, in bold above:
*"Prioritizes listening to others' existing desires over imposing their own
voice."*

It has no subject. Every sentence around it names one — "The user", "The
person" — and this one begins with a bare verb. In an essay a reader would
supply the subject from the previous sentence, which is what elision is for. Here
the previous sentence is about color bending as visual protest, written on a
different day from a different snippet, and it lends nothing. The sentence is
grammatical only if you assume continuity that the document does not have.

That is a pile's signature: an artifact whose parts were written as if something
would join them, placed next to each other by an ordering that joins nothing.

The subject drift is corpus-wide, not a one-off: "The user" opens 59 claims,
"The person" 28, "The author" 2, and a bare "They" most of the rest. Nothing
made 144 independent calls agree on who the wiki is about. Eight ranges say only
`generally` or `in general`, which tells a reader nothing about when the claim
holds — and Range is mandatory precisely so a claim cannot float free of its
occasion.

None of this is a rejection of the slice. The mechanics did everything they
promised: mandatory Range on all 144, every cite resolving, nothing deleted, no
confidence number anywhere, a status vocabulary that refused to confirm what it
could not confirm. What the read shows is that an essay needs claims that share
evidence, and this corpus has none. **The reading surface should be judged again
on a corpus where snippets are cited twice.** The plan's own second reason for
deferring the typography ("The UI decision", plan L57ff) was that designing an
essay renderer against imagined claims repeats the accretion failure the first
wireframe pass made. It is now clear that 144 real claims with no shared
evidence are, for that purpose, still imagined claims.

## 16. What this changes

Each corrective is tied to the number that earns it. Nothing here is a
speculative improvement.

**1. A completion without a budget is a single point of failure — done.**
Earned by the 76-minute stall (§13) against a 29s baseline. Ticket 086, shipped
as `38c4a8a` mid-drain: 180s `AbortController` per call, `max_tokens: 16384` on
every request. The seven runs after it, 84 readings, zero incidents. This one is
closed; it is listed because it is the exemplar — the number was measured, the
ticket was minted, the fix was built, and the fix was verified by the remainder
of the same run.

**2. The claim-quality correctives go to the harvester, not the Clerk.**
Earned by §3's identical columns: 144 facets in, 144 facets out, zero drift. The
Clerk cannot improve a reading. The subject drift ("The user" 59 / "The person"
28 / "The author" 2) and the contentless ranges (`generally` ×7) are mint-prompt
and reading-quality problems. The mint prompt should fix the grammatical person,
and Range should be rejected when it names no occasion. **New ticket, and note
it must not become an op rejection**: a claim with a weak Range is still a claim,
so the corrective belongs in the prompt and in lint, not in the write contract.

**3. The op contract is measured on one branch of six.** Earned by §4: 144 MINT,
0 of everything else, on a corpus where §7 shows no reading could have called
for another op. Nothing to fix, and something to *not* conclude — do not read
"0% rejection" as "the model holds the six-op contract". Re-measure the
rejection rate on a corpus where snippets are cited twice, which is the seeding
slice's output. Until then the rate is a fact about MINT.

**4. The candidate pool is one channel wide.** Earned by §8: lexical proposed
100 pairs, referent 0, embedding 0-by-shadow. §9's single opposed pair is a
lexical false positive. The lexical channel is the one the eval measured at 0/8
recall on paraphrase, and it is currently the only thing feeding a live gate.
The embedding channel's shadow record now argues for graduation — 811 of 1,563
pairs cross a sitting (§8) — and the honest sequencing is: graduate the
embedding channel *before* re-reading Q-49's precision, because judging the gate
on pairs a 3-word exact-match index chose measures the index, not the gate.

**5. The structural guard verifies provenance and cannot verify aboutness.**
Earned by §10: the re-measure quoted `worked on making`, an exact substring that
passed Q-46's check and is not a proposition. Q-46 stays — the alternative is a
model self-report, which is worse. The corrective is narrower: require the
quoted pole to be a complete clause, which is a mechanical check on the fragment
rather than a judgment about it. **New ticket.**

**6. The re-measure never reached a person, so the ratio the plan cared most
about is still unmeasured.** Earned by §10's `pending` entry. The direct price of
a self-reported boolean (`unverified-confirmation` versus `dissolved-on-answer`)
needs one answered re-measure. That is not a code change; it is a note for the
next real sitting — draw the queue.

**7. Cost accounting, for planning rather than for fixing.** The chain took
3h 51m wall (§2). The measured floor is 73.7 minutes: 12 runs × 6m 42s at the
post-086 clean rate (§13). The gap is 76 minutes of stall plus three
process restarts. Two line items are worth naming because they are not sweep
time:

- **Opener minting cost 10m 36s of one run.** The 19:21:32Z boot minted 8
  openers, finishing at 19:32:08Z, before the sweep began. SOURCE:
  `run-started` and `opener-minted` lines. That is 14% of the measured floor
  spent in a job unrelated to the drain, and it is invisible in the per-run
  averages because it happened once.
- **The candidates job never had work at boot.** It ran only after claims
  existed, and was skipped four times by 076's `index-current` gate (§13). This
  is the gate behaving correctly, recorded here so a future reader does not
  mistake the skips for a broken job.

**8. Two shadow thresholds are measuring the wrong thing, cheaply.**
`lint.godNodeFanout=12` fires on every facet that exceeds 12 claims (§8), and
since facets are a closed vocabulary of eight, every common facet will exceed 12
on any real corpus. It is measuring corpus size, not fan-out. **New ticket:**
either scope the fanout to referents, where a god-node is a real risk (T11's
open question), or scale the threshold with the corpus.

## Appendix — what a reader should not conclude

Three numbers in this document look like verdicts and are not.

**"Zero rejections" is not "the model can do this job."** It is "the model
proposed MINT 144 times and MINT was correct 144 times." §4 and §7.

**"Zero contradictions opened" is not a failure of the contradiction
pipeline.** The pipeline ran to the exact point where it required a person and
stopped, which is Q-30's specified behaviour. §11.

**"144 unconfirmed" is not a bug.** Q-50 requires two independent cites from
distinct sittings and no claim in this corpus has two cites at all. The
vocabulary is refusing to confirm what it cannot confirm. §7.
