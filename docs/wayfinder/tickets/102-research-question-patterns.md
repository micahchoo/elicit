## Question

Micah, 2026-08-03: "the 'you wrote X — …?' pattern is tiresome… something
that derives from source text, not just restates it. One particular way is
to create scenarios from source text. Think of other patterns as well.
The questions are the heart of the product."

Today the verbatim-quote-back is the ONLY composed surface form (Q-12 made
the quote structural; juxtaposition, still-true, mirror all reuse it).
Survey the derivation patterns — question forms *built from* the person's
material:

- **Scenario/vignette**: a concrete hypothetical assembled from their own
  episodes and constructs (vignette methodology; CDM's probe families;
  situational-judgment items).
- **Counterfactual twist**: their episode with one variable altered.
- **Dilemma from their own poles**: a Construct is pole + contrast — build
  the forced choice from both.
- **Instance-testing**: construct a specific case and ask if their general
  claim covers it ("would that include …?").
- **Externalizing** (narrative therapy): the problem as a character built
  from their words. **Miracle/exception and scaling questions**
  (solution-focused). **Sentence stems** from their phrasing. **Metaphor
  extension** (Clean Language — derives while quoting; the least
  contaminating). **Question reversal** ("ask me the question you'd want
  answered").

Re-read before new reading: research-question-policy.md §1 (probe
taxonomies), §8 (Socratic/creative-disruption), §12 in Synthesis-(d)
(anti-selection: Clean Language's paraphrase ban — every derivation
deliberately introduces content, which is exactly what needs licensing).
The doc's own Gaps list flags unread items that bear directly: AInterviewer
(ACL 2026 demo), SparkMe (arXiv:2602.21136), Xiao et al. 2020 (TOCHI,
adaptive probing effect sizes). Vignette methodology and projective
techniques have no section yet — new territory.
