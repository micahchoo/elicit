## Question

From the 2026-08-02 HANDOFF review.

Diachronic ("you changed") and synchronic ("you hold both") are the clean
cases and both have machinery. The review argues the common case is neither:
a person who is right in both domains and has not drawn the boundary. Naming
that boundary is plausibly the highest-value thing the system can produce,
and the design currently gives it one sentence as "the missing work" — it
falls out of the Contradiction pipeline as a residual.

Grill: a question form that takes two poles and asks for the DISCRIMINATING
CONDITION — where does the first hold, where does the second, what tells them
apart. Whether it is a QuestionForm, a Protocol instrument, or a third
Contradiction type; what it cites (both poles, Q-12-composed); and what the
answer becomes — a third claim that supersedes neither pole, most likely,
which is a shape the op contract (Q-29) does not have a verb for yet.

## Resolution (2026-08-02) — Q-54

The ticket says the answer "is a shape the op contract (Q-29) does not have a
verb for yet." That is wrong, and the reason is structural.

**Q-21 already made Range mandatory on every Claim** — Bateson, no context-free
trait claims. Range IS the boundary. Two claims whose bodies oppose but whose
Ranges do not are not in contradiction; they are correctly scoped. The un-drawn
boundary is the case where both Ranges are identical or both vague. So the
answer to "where does the first hold, where does the second" is a narrowed Range
on each pole — `SUPERSEDE` with reason, which Q-29 already has. Not a third
Contradiction type. Not a new Protocol instrument. Not a new verb.

**The signal is already generated and thrown away.** When a re-measure comes
back "at work I do X, with my kids I do Y", that is the highest-value sentence
the pipeline will ever produce, and today it lands in `dissolved-on-answer` — a
string — and the candidate is archived. The boundary was named, in the user's
own prose, harvested as a Snippet, and discarded as a non-event.

### Door 1 — the pipeline branch

- A fifth outcome `range-discriminated`, consequence: one `SUPERSEDE` per pole
  with a narrowed Range and reason `range-discriminated:<candidateId>`.
- Cites both poles' snippets plus the discriminating answer's.
- Q-53 forces that answer into a different sitting from both poles, so under
  Q-50 the refined claims are born **`evidenced`**. The best claims in the wiki
  arrive with the strongest status mechanically.
- The question comes AFTER the re-measure, only on the dissolution branch, never
  instead of it — "when does X hold versus Y" presupposes both poles and leads
  the witness. Often no second question is needed, because the dissolving answer
  named the condition itself.

### Door 2 — the lint finding, and it ships FIRST

Door 1 inherits every recall problem in Q-52. Hanging the highest-value output
in the system off its flakiest machinery is not acceptable. Door 2 has no model
and no candidate pool in the path — see
[Discriminating questions from lint](060-discriminating-question-lint.md).
