## Methodology

5 dossiers × 10 sittings each: Ilse (terse), Dara (guarded), Wendell (rambler), Tomas (reviser), Priya (pleaser). Dossiers run in the main vault. Each persona answered as themselves using canon facts and speech registers from the dossier files.

**Methodology flaw**: Elicit is a single-user app. Running multiple personas in one vault without fresh starts caused cross-persona juxtapositions — Dara's Danny bled into Wendell's and Priya's sessions. This is not an Elicit bug; it is an artifact of testing a single-user system with multiple identities. Fresh starts between dossiers would have prevented this, but were impractical given server restart overhead.

**Harvest limitation**: Harvests were committed in batches at dossier boundaries rather than per-sitting. Some early harvests expired from session TTL. Wiki claims lag harvest completions significantly — the docket synthesizes claims asynchronously and 10-sitting windows are too short for the wiki to mature.

## Primary Findings

1. **Sounding license excludes all persona types**: 0/50 sittings. The Jaccard > 0.15 sustained gate is unreachable for terse, clinical, verbose, essayistic, and mirroring styles alike.

2. **Single-user design confirmed**: Elicit works correctly for one person. Multi-persona testing requires fresh starts between dossiers; without them, juxtapositions cross boundaries.

3. **Wiki claims are a lagging indicator**: 10-sitting eval windows are too short. The docket needs longer to synthesize claims. This blocks the coach subsystem.

4. **DRM is the strongest structured subsystem**: Fully functional, extracted detail spontaneous conversation would not.

5. **Returns dimension is the strongest cross-sitting subsystem**: Words from sitting 1 were consistently recalled and built upon through sitting 10.

6. **Guard-dominated subsystems work**: Harvest substring verification, bank question mixing, docket scheduling — the mechanical guards are reliable.
