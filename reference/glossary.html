Reference · How Elicit Works

Plain-English Glossary

Every project term the lessons use, in ordinary language. The precise, canonical definitions live in CONTEXT.md — this page is the on-ramp, not the authority. When a lesson uses a capitalized word that feels like jargon, it's here.

Grouped by what they belong to, not alphabetically — the groups are half the understanding.

The stuff that gets saved

Snippet
A saved passage of your own words — exact, dated, never edited again. The atom everything else is built from. Editing one creates a new version; the old version stays as evidence of a past you. CONTEXT.md "Snippet"
Bud
A fragment of yours that failed one of the save-worthiness tests (for example, it can't be understood on its own). Kept anyway, with its failures recorded — each failure is a future question, because answering it would mature the Bud into a real Snippet. CONTEXT.md "Bud"
Facet
A label for what kind of self-knowledge a Snippet shows: a specific datable memory, a general fact about you, a value, an intention, a theory about your own causes, and so on. CONTEXT.md "Facet"
Construct
One of the Facet kinds, borrowed from personal-construct psychology: a personal dimension you judge things on, defined by a pole and its contrast pole ("disciplined vs. scattered"). A pole alone — "half-Construct" — is incomplete by definition, which is why the app asks "what's the opposite of this, for you?" CONTEXT.md "Facet"
Vault
The archive on disk: plain markdown files in their own git repo. Snippets, transcripts, the Wiki, the question Queue all live here. README "Your words stay yours"
Transcript
The full record of one conversation — every question and every answer, append-only, never edited. CONTEXT.md "Transcript"

The model of you

Wiki
The app's continuously revised model of you — short sentences of agent prose ("claims"), each citing the exact Snippet versions it rests on. You can read and edit it; it never gets to write your words. CONTEXT.md "Wiki"
Claim
One sentence in the Wiki, with mandatory citations to Snippets and a mandatory range (the context where it holds). No citation, no claim; no confidence scores anywhere, ever. CONTEXT.md "Claim"
Contradiction
A recorded tension between two things you said. Typed: either both assert the present (genuine tension → a question), or you changed over time (the change itself is the finding — nothing to resolve). CONTEXT.md "Contradiction"

The conversation

Sitting
One sit-down with the app, from declaring your time and energy to the closing questions. The code calls it a "session." CONTEXT.md "Sitting"
Mode
What you declare at the start of a Sitting: how much time you have, how much energy, what the session is about. It constrains what's askable now — a deep question waits for a Mode that fits it. CONTEXT.md "Mode"
Protocol
A question's category, defined by what it takes to answer well — a memory question needs retrieval time, a comparison question needs materials in front of you. Protocols are data files, not hardcoded types. CONTEXT.md "Protocol"
Harvesting
Breaking your prose into candidate Snippets. The app proposes cuts — exact substrings of what you typed, nothing reworded — and you approve, trim, discard, or restate each one. CONTEXT.md "Harvesting"
Content-free answer
A flat, closed answer — "not sure," "I guess" — that gives the app nothing to work with. Detected by a simple heuristic, and used as a signal to change tack rather than push. src/elicitor/answer-shape.ts

Where questions come from

Queue
The durable to-ask list. Questions minted by background work, deferred by you, or declared by you wait here between Sittings. CONTEXT.md "Queue"
Bank
A curated file of opener questions (data/question-bank.jsonl) — hand-picked, not generated. The fallback when nothing better is licensed. CONTEXT.md "Question Bank"
Red Light
A feature of your last answer that invites a follow-up: an odd or loaded word, a person or thing you named but didn't explain, an abstraction with no concrete memory under it, a pole without its contrast, a cause claimed without the event. CONTEXT.md "Red Light"
Resonance
The every-turn search of your archive for past Snippets that echo or clash with what you just said — so the archive participates in the live conversation. CONTEXT.md "Resonance"
Juxtaposition
The question a Resonance hit licenses: what you just said and what you once said, quoted side by side, dated — "same thing?" The app never says which one is right. CONTEXT.md "Juxtaposition"
Still-true revisit
An old statement of yours, re-asked after it has aged — always worded differently than the first time, so you're not just pattern-matching your own past answer. CONTEXT.md "Question Source"
Randomizer
The serendipity mechanism: it resurfaces forgotten Snippets or draws from decks you curated. It shuffles what exists — it never invents, and the agent can't veto its draws. CONTEXT.md "Randomizer"
Expedition
A question with homework: it sends you out to read or investigate something over days, then asks for your reflection when you're back. CONTEXT.md "Expedition"
Guard
A mechanical check a composed question must pass before it reaches you — is the quote really your exact words, is it actually a question, is it not a near-duplicate. Guards can only reject; they never fix. src/elicitor/guards.ts

Structured deep-dives ("instruments")

Sounding
A consent-gated chain of questions where each answer becomes the seed of the next — drilling down toward what you actually believe. Runs 8–12 rungs, stops when you converge or hit the cap, and at checkpoints offers: continue, park it (depth kept for later), or another day. CONTEXT.md "Sounding"
DRM (Day Reconstruction Method)
A borrowed research instrument: you list yesterday's episodes (start times only), then each episode gets the same four short probes — where, what, with whom, how it felt. Tiles across a day where a Sounding drills down into a belief. docs/decisions Q-85, src/drm/
Rung
One question-answer step of a Sounding's descent. The next rung's question must quote a piece of the previous answer — that's what makes it a chain and not a topic drift. src/sounding/ladder.ts
Park
Pausing an instrument mid-descent with all progress kept. A parked Sounding or DRM waits in the Queue until you explicitly resume it. src/sounding/park.ts

Things made from Snippets

Piece
An essay assembled from your Snippets and nothing else — your sentences, your order, no generated filler. Never "finished," only set down and resumable. CONTEXT.md "Piece"
Arrangement
One ordering of a Piece's Snippets — the same material can stack as chronology, argument, or contrast. Cheap to keep alternatives of. CONTEXT.md "Arrangement"
Gap (in an Arrangement)
A visible empty slot where no Snippet bridges a leap between two others. Backed by a queued question; never filled by agent prose. CONTEXT.md "Gap"

Background machinery

Clerk
The agent's background role — it does the between-sittings work and is the only writer to the Wiki. Also the name of the slower, more careful of the two model roles. CONTEXT.md "Clerk", src/llm.ts
Docket
The Clerk's standing task list: re-read new Snippets, detect contradictions, mint questions, run the sweeps. Finished work waits for you on the interface — no task ever contacts you. CONTEXT.md "Docket"
Shadow mode
This project's rollout discipline: a new mechanism that would act on its own judgment first runs in shadow — it evaluates and logs what it would do, but does nothing — until the log earns it a graduation to live. Mechanisms that only offer (you can decline in a word) skip shadow and ship live. docs/decisions Q-35, Q-62
Sweep
A Docket job that walks the whole archive looking for one kind of thing — stale pins, dormant Pieces, unfilled gaps — and acts on (or in shadow, logs) each hit. src/clerk/docket.ts
Gazetteer
The index of people and things you've mentioned, built by the Clerk — so the app can notice "you've mentioned this person eight times" and ask outward from that. src/clerk/gazetteer.ts
Territory skeleton / Atlas
Two kinds of private checklist for what hasn't been asked about yet. A skeleton is a structured prerequisite map of a domain you know (this concept builds on that one); an atlas is a deliberately crude flat map of what a life contains. Both track only "asked or not" — never a score, never shown to you as a progress bar. docs/decisions Q-79, src/ktg/
Lineage mirror
A shadow-mode sweep that may (once live) quote your own usage facts back to you — "you wrote this in March, and touched it again yesterday" — only ever in service of testing a specific claim, never as commentary on your habits. docs/decisions Q-83, src/clerk/lineage-mirror.ts
Marginalia
The agent's margin notes — anything the agent writes about your material. Structurally barred from ever entering a Piece. CONTEXT.md "Marginalia"
Mint
The verb for background work creating something durable — a question minted into the Queue, a claim minted into the Wiki. Minting is always logged, and most minting paths are capped per run. src/clerk/docket.ts, src/clerk/mint.ts
Annotation
A margin note the Clerk attaches to a specific Snippet — a dangling reference it noticed, an intention with a time horizon. Marginalia-class: about your words, never inside them. src/clerk/annotate.ts
Consolidation (the "Cover")
Folding old transcripts into one-line summaries so unbounded history can fit a fixed reading budget — newest sessions verbatim, older ranges as single lines, raw text always recoverable. One summary per docket run, max. docs/adr/0002-three-layer-memory.md, src/memory/cover.ts
Watermark
A git marker recording "the last state the Clerk looked at" — so background jobs can skip work when nothing changed. A commit, not a timestamp. src/index/watermark.ts

Wiki upkeep

Reading
The Clerk's recorded interpretation of one Snippet — what kind of self-knowledge it shows, what it might evidence. The raw material claims are proposed from; every Reading cites its Snippet. src/clerk/wiki-jobs.ts
Op
One of six edits the model may propose against the Wiki: mint a claim, update, merge, supersede, archive, or keep. Proposals only — a mechanical gate applies or refuses each one. src/wiki/ops.ts, src/clerk/mint.ts
Lint (of the Wiki)
Mechanical checks over the whole claim graph — orphaned claims, stale citations, suspiciously similar pairs. Two kinds of finding become queued questions for you; the rest just render. src/wiki/lint.ts
Clash candidate
A pair of claims or snippets that might contradict, surfaced by mechanical channels (a shared phrase, a shared name) and then judged. A judged opposition only survives if both poles are exact quotes of what was shown. src/wiki/clash.ts, src/clerk/contradiction.ts
Terms here are the ones lessons 1–2 use. When a future lesson needs a term that isn't here, adding it here is part of writing that lesson — this page is the contract.
